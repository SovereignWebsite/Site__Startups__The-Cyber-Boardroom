Cyber risk, in the board's language

Board packs your directors can read, and evidence they can check

The Cyber Boardroom started in 2024 to help boards understand cyber risk1,2. In November 2026 it restarts around one product: the board pack3. A pack puts the risks the organisation accepts, what each AI agent is allowed to do, and the evidence behind both in front of the board, kept in an encrypted vault.

See what goes in a board pack Who we are

Why now

In February 2026 the founder wrote that The Cyber Boardroom was "on life support, not because the idea was wrong, but because it was ahead of the infrastructure needed to deliver it"4. The missing pieces now exist in the companies around it:

  • Encrypted vaults

    SG/Send gives "data rooms with zero-knowledge encryption" for "sensitive board materials"4, and each board member can get "a personalised view encrypted to their key"5.

  • Agent Behaviour Policies

    RiskMandate writes down, for each AI agent, what it can actually do and what you authorised, and puts something in the way of the difference6.

  • Risk acceptance

    A risk register view where "simulated acceptance" stays "indelibly distinct from real", naming "roles not named individuals"7.

One company underneath

The Cyber Boardroom Limited, UK-registered, "is today the commercial vehicle behind sgit.ai and RiskMandate.ai"8. Its GitHub organisation also holds the code of SG/Send9,10. How the pieces connect.

Where it stands

The board pack is being built now. There are no prices or customers to show yet, and this site will not invent any. The 2024 platform, with Athena, the AI cyber advisor, is still online at thecyberboardroom.com1; read the history and what Athena was.
